# Sources and lessons

## Precedents

- Newsmap (2004), MoMA: https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2011/talktome/objects/146231/ . Uses topic-colored rectangles sized by coverage. Lesson: hierarchy can show relationships and editorial attention, but size should not imply truth or importance automatically.
- NewsStand, UMD paper: https://www.cs.umd.edu/~hjs/pubs/newsstand-acmgis2008.pdf . Spatial RSS clustering and pan/zoom. Lesson: orientation and stable context matter; this prototype uses topic neighborhoods instead of geographic claims.
- Pad++ HTML browser: https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/hcil/pad%2B%2B/tour/html1.html and https://www.cs.umd.edu/projects/hcil/pad%2B%2B/tour/conclusion.html . Linked documents appear beside parents and retain peripheral context. This directly informed unfolding without a separate full-screen page.
- Flipboard layout engineering: https://about.flipboard.com/engineering/layout-in-flipboard-for-web-and-windows/ . Human-designed layout templates inform the editorial rhythm rather than arbitrary masonry.

These lessons are design interpretations, not claims that these products solved every usability issue.

Each record retains the feed URL, publisher site, feed directory/evidence link, configured topic scope, publisher-base label, and descriptive source type. `dist/feed.json` contains collected headlines, brief plain-text excerpts (bounded to 350 characters), original article URLs/dates, and per-feed collection status. It contains no imported third-party full articles or newly downloaded third-party image assets.

## Endless spatial interaction and Street View (05)

The current canvas repeats its finite, attributed collection across unbounded positive/negative cell coordinates. This supersedes edition 04's finite mosaic; unique source-item counts remain visible. Replication preserves each item's source identity and credits. No additional recipes, reports, or business photos are fabricated to fill the world. Opened details expand inside their cell, and local span packing reserves room for them.

Google Street View business covers use the documented [Street View Static API](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/request-streetview), [metadata endpoint](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/metadata), and [API key setup](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/get-api-key). The server checks for a nearby panorama, calculates the camera heading toward the existing business coordinates, and streams a bounded image response. It does not save or cache Google's images/metadata, put image URLs into saved items, or include Google content/keys in snapshots. The client uses uncropped imagery so returned Google copyright and attribution remain present. [Google policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/policies) apply.

No Google Maps API key was configured during implementation; provider behavior was tested with fixtures, and the UI's honest unavailable state was browser-checked. Venue cover initials have been replaced with this Street View cover path/fallback. Food imagery remains explicitly illustrative and appears on hover, rather than masquerading as a business exterior. The geographic map continues to use separately attributed OpenStreetMap data and tiles.

## News photographs, video and locator geography

The news collector retains media supplied in publisher RSS/Atom feeds: Media RSS content/thumbnails/credits, enclosures, and supported image/video references in feed HTML. Remote photographs remain on the publisher's servers, carry the supplied credits/captions and story link, and may become unavailable. Broadsheet does not acquire ownership or a general redistribution license by reading a feed. Explicit Media RSS restrictions suppress the media. Native video files and validated YouTube/Vimeo embeds play only in opened cards; no autoplay or arbitrary publisher HTML is inserted. See the [Media RSS specification](https://www.rssboard.org/media-rss).

The NASA source additionally includes a small, dated selection from the [NASA Image and Video Library API](https://images.nasa.gov/docs/images.nasa.gov_api_docs.pdf). Video files and poster images remain NASA-hosted, and library publication dates, creator/center credits and original source links are retained. NASA's [images and media guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) applies; third-party material is not assumed public domain.

Regional locator silhouettes derive from **Natural Earth 1:110m Admin 0 countries**, maintained by Tom Patterson, Nathaniel Vaughn Kelso and contributors, under [Natural Earth's public-domain terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). The source dataset is from the [maintainer's repository](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/master/geojson/ne_110m_admin_0_countries.geojson). `dist/news-geography.json` records retrieval date, original SHA-256, source URL and modifications: an equirectangular projection rounded to 0.1 SVG units with reduced attributes. The UI attributes Natural Earth. These are simplified editorial locators, not navigational maps or boundary determinations.

Locators match explicit country/territory names and a small documented set of geographic headline aliases. They show regional context, not a verified incident location. Publisher GeoRSS coordinates retain their provenance and are also not presented as verified event pins. Related headlines share at least two significant headline terms within seven days when dates exist; their actual headlines, source names and shared terms are shown. This is not verified same-event clustering, a factual verdict, or a bias rating.

Photo similarity uses retained 64-bit perceptual fingerprints of the main feed/archive images, with whole-image and center-crop variants. The optional `news_fingerprints.py` analyzer reads bounded public thumbnails/local retained images and stores only hashes, not downloaded image bytes. Approximate visual matches suppress repeated covers in the overview; a matching photo plus substantial headline overlap within a week can collect separate publisher reports under one card, without merging their text or source metadata. This does not certify the same event or change image rights. Full source media and credits stay inside each opened article. Unsupported/unavailable image formats fall back to URL identity.

## On-demand article reading

Configured publishers' public article pages can be read inside unfolded cards. A bounded, anonymous request to the original HTTPS page extracts visible article paragraphs, headings and lists using Cloudflare HTMLRewriter, retaining supplied bylines and the source link. Original HTML/scripts never enter the application DOM. Known subscription flags and publisher preview restrictions suppress extraction; denial/challenge pages are not bypassed. Every redirect stays inside the publisher's explicit host group. This does not acquire publisher rights, extract subscription-only content, or publish a permanent full-text archive. Text is returned only on request with no-store response headers; the application shares a small, temporary memory cache between repeated card instances. News photos and videos keep their existing credits and original hosting.

Al Jazeera video reports use the official Brightcove player URL supplied in the page's public `VideoObject` metadata, restricted to its published player account. Short video descriptions are presented as video descriptions rather than failed full-text articles. Live coverage follows the same-origin AMP alternate explicitly linked by a successfully fetched public page, retaining visible update headings and text. The reader labels these as the latest published updates, offers refresh, and limits their memory cache to one minute; this is not a complete historical live-blog archive. Access denials and subscription restrictions never trigger alternate-page requests. BBC was removed from the active catalog, snapshot and gateway on November 11, 2026 because the deployed reader was denied. It is no longer requested by Broadsheet.

### Performance and verification limits

The collector stays at six simultaneous feeds, 40 entries/feed, 5 MB/feed and 18-second network timeouts with failed-source retention. No whole-feed refresh is triggered by article opening. Full text is loaded only for opened cards. Client article concurrency is three with twelve queued, repeated requests coalesce, and thirty settled records are retained in memory. Successful Global Voices/EFF articles use an existing Cloudflare edge cache for fifteen minutes; failures are not cached, and a cache outage falls back to the original. Cache entries are data-center-local, not a global quota counter. Existing Guardian caching and all other adapters are preserved.

Regression checks cover URL/redirect boundaries, BBC rejection, author/translator credit, license/link validation, third-party media omission, separate rights notices, final paragraphs, edge cache hits/failure handling, and a 40-open burst with peak concurrency three. The normal lightweight test suite and native Worker HTMLRewriter fixtures pass. These are bounded functional/performance safeguards, not a production-scale traffic certification or a guarantee against provider quota limits. The static public edition must still be rebuilt/refreshed to update discovery.


Guardian's current [developer access](https://open-platform.theguardian.com/access/) is noncommercial, **one request per second and 500 requests per day**, and [terms](https://www.theguardian.com/open-platform/terms-and-conditions) require cached text to be refreshed or deleted within 24 hours. The existing 15-minute edge and memory TTLs remain below that freshness window, with no persistent article archive or stale-forever fallback. The per-client gateway throttle is not a global Guardian quota limiter. A higher-traffic release would need a suitable Guardian license/allowance and a shared quota scheduler before claiming that scale; no new plan, resource or cost was enabled here.


## Public meme feeds

The Memes space reads public posts with media attachments from six feeds: [memes / Lemmy World](https://lemmy.world/c/memes), [me_irl / Lemmy World](https://lemmy.world/c/me_irl), [programmer_humor / programming.dev](https://programming.dev/c/programmer_humor), [#funny / mastodon.social](https://mastodon.social/tags/funny), [#memes / mastodon.social](https://mastodon.social/tags/memes), and [#memes / mastodon.online](https://mastodon.online/tags/memes).

Lemmy uses its [public post-list API](https://join-lemmy.org/docs/contributors/04-api.html); Mastodon uses its [hashtag timeline API](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/methods/timelines/#tag) and source-supplied [media attachments](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/entities/MediaAttachment/). Metadata is cached for ten minutes, up to 60 response entries. No credentials, HTML scraping, arbitrary URL proxy, media downloading, or rehosting is involved. Requests omit credentials. Media and poster URLs must be HTTPS; private/local hostnames and active URL schemes are rejected.

Only public, non-sensitive Mastodon posts and non-deleted/non-removed/non-NSFW Lemmy posts are admitted. Labels are provider supplied and cannot guarantee suitability. Video, looping video, GIF images, and multiple image attachments retain their native type and alternative text where supplied. Public feed availability does not grant a media reuse license: every normalized item retains its original post, contributor attribution, and an explicit unknown-reuse-rights caveat. The checked-in snapshot contains metadata only; collect it with `node collect_memes.cjs`. A federation-aware media key collapses identical original attachment URLs, but visually identical reuploads at different URLs can still appear.

Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and Reddit are not connected catalog sources. No claim is made to scrape or bypass their access controls.
